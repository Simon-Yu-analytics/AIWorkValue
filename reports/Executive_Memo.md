# Decision memo: AI writing assistance

**Recommendation:** fund a bounded, measured writing-workflow pilot. Defer a broad investment commitment until verification time, eligible task volume and real costs are observed.

**Evidence.** An independent reconstruction of the Noy–Zhang public experiment retains 453 participants. In the non-fixed-time arms, adjusted assignment contrasts among available cases are -10.34 minutes (95% interval -12.58 to -8.10; N=346) and +0.70 writing-grade points on a 1–7 scale (+0.47 to +0.93; N=352). Change-score, objective-duration and alternative grading checks support the direction of these results.

**Interpretation.** The historical experiment concerns short professional-writing tasks and early-2023 technology. It does not establish current-model performance, factual accuracy, whole-job productivity or company profit. Differential attrition limits a full-sample interpretation. The analysis uses assigned access, not a selected group of actual AI users.

**Who benefits?** Exploratory comparisons suggest larger quality gains at lower baseline writing scores. The time interaction remains imprecise. Use this finding to investigate onboarding needs, not to rank employees or determine employment outcomes.

**Commercial case.** Under the documented base assumptions, 100 offered seats across a 200-person organization produce approximately $698/month of net capacity value. The conditional operating seat-cost ceiling is $31.98/month. Statistical time-effect uncertainty alone changes net monthly value to approximately -$610 to +$2,007; uncertainty in transferability and costs is additional. Verification overhead above about 2.60 minutes/task removes the positive base operating case.

**Next decision.** Identify recurring, self-contained writing tasks. Measure full time to accepted output, blinded quality, verification/rework, real task frequency and offered-seat usage. Recalculate with actual cost quotes. Expand only if the conservative case is acceptable and released time has a concrete use. Released capacity is not automatically a cash saving.

Source: Noy and Zhang, Science (2023), DOI 10.1126/science.adh2586; author replication package OSF `xd7qw`. This project contributes reanalysis and a commercial extension; it did not conduct the original experiment.
